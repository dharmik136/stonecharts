# Prospect Sourcing and Outreach Plan

The interview script ([`SC-PROD-006`](prospect-qualification-scorecard.md)) and cost
worksheet ([`SC-PROD-009`](cost-quantification-worksheet.md)) answer "what do I ask
once someone agrees to talk." This document answers the question in front of that:
"who do I ask, and how do I get them to say yes to 15 minutes." It is a method, not a
promise — nothing here guarantees response rates; it is a starting sourcing and
outreach approach to run, measure, and revise.

## Phasing toward the validation gate

[`SC-PROD-003`](visual-integrity-strategy.md)'s gate is 20 interviews, ≥10 recurring
problems, ≥6 quantified costs, ≥4 real fixtures, ≥3 technical proofs, ≥2 paid pilots,
1 conversion. That is not one phase, it is five, run partly in parallel once the
pipeline is moving:

1. **Build the list** (ongoing, front-loaded) — enough names that 20 completed
   interviews is realistic given response rates, not enough names that quality drops.
2. **Outreach** (ongoing) — get 15 minutes on the calendar.
3. **Interview** (rolling, as calls land) — run the `SC-PROD-006` script, fill
   `SC-PROD-009` live.
4. **Technical proof of value** (after ~10-15 interviews have identified the
   strongest-fit prospects) — run StoneVerify ([`SC-QUAL-004`](../quality/stoneverify-quickstart.md))
   against a real fixture the prospect supplies.
5. **Paid pilot conversation** (after a technical proof succeeds) — propose a scoped,
   paid pilot to the best-fit candidates.

Do not wait for phase 1 to "finish" before starting phase 2, and do not wait for all
20 interviews before starting phase 4 with the strongest early candidates. Run them
overlapping.

## Phase 1: Build the list

**Target criteria** (per DEC-017's segment): insurance reporting and actuarial
platforms. Within that, prioritize organizations where:

- Reports or actuarial exhibits are generated by more than one internal team or
  system (raises the odds of a real cross-language/cross-tool drift problem existing).
- There is a recurring, dated reporting cycle (monthly/quarterly filings, board
  reporting, regulatory submissions) — recurring cycles create recurring pain,
  which is more fundable than a one-off complaint.
- The company is large enough to have a distinct platform/infrastructure team
  separate from the actuarial/reporting team (so there is a real "two teams, two
  toolchains" seam to find drift in).

**Target roles to find** (matches `SC-PROD-006`'s three personas):

- Platform/infrastructure engineering lead or manager
- Actuarial reporting owner, or an audit/compliance director with reporting-systems
  scope
- BI/reporting product manager

**Sourcing channels, roughly in order of expected response quality:**

1. **Existing network first.** Anyone already one introduction away from an insurance
   reporting or actuarial platform team beats any cold channel. Check LinkedIn first-
   and second-degree connections for the target roles before doing anything else.
2. **LinkedIn direct search / Sales Navigator**, filtered by title (the three roles
   above) and industry (Insurance). A regulated-reporting angle also shows up under
   adjacent titles: "Actuarial Systems," "Regulatory Reporting," "Financial Reporting
   Technology."
3. **InsurTech and actuarial communities**: industry Slack/Discord groups, LOMA and
   SOA (Society of Actuaries) member forums, local InsurTech meetup groups. These
   skew toward people already thinking about tooling problems, which is exactly the
   qualification signal the interview script is built to find.
4. **Conference attendee/speaker lists** for actuarial or insurance-technology
   conferences (even without attending, speaker and panelist lists are public and
   name people actively working the problem).
5. **Cold outbound as the last resort**, not the first channel — it has the lowest
   response rate of the five and should be reserved for filling gaps once the first
   four are exhausted.

**List-size target:** aim for 60-80 names to reach 20 completed interviews,
assuming roughly a 25-30% conversation rate from a warm-ish channel and lower from
cold outbound. Track the funnel (contacted → responded → booked → completed) from
day one so the real conversion rate replaces this assumption quickly.

## Phase 2: Outreach templates

Keep every message short, specific, and framed around their cost, not the product.
Do not attach the one-pager to a first message — it reads as a pitch before they've
agreed to talk. Send it only after they've agreed to a conversation, if at all.

**LinkedIn / warm-intro message:**

> Hi [name] — I'm working on a narrow question in insurance reporting: when the same
> chart or figure is generated by two different systems or teams (say, a Python
> analysis pipeline and a production report service), how often does that actually
> cause a real problem for you — a review question, a refiling, a client complaint?
> Not selling anything on this message, genuinely trying to learn if this is a real
> cost or a non-issue. Worth 15 minutes sometime in the next couple of weeks?

**Cold email subject line:** `Quick question about report-rendering consistency`

**Cold email body:**

> [Name] — reaching out because of your role in [reporting/actuarial systems] at
> [company]. I'm researching a specific, narrow problem: whether generating the same
> chart or report figure across more than one backend system or team ever causes a
> real cost — an audit question, a delayed filing, a client complaint. If this has
> never come up for your team, that's a useful answer too. If you have 15 minutes in
> the next two weeks, I'd like to ask a few questions and share nothing in return
> except what I learn from others in the same seat. No pitch on this email.

**If they ask "what is this for" before agreeing:** be honest — this is discovery for
a company building conformance tooling in this space, and the goal of the call is to
find out whether the problem is real and costly enough to be worth solving, not to
sell them something on the first call.

## Phase 3: Running the interview

Use [`SC-PROD-006`](prospect-qualification-scorecard.md) verbatim. Fill
[`SC-PROD-009`](cost-quantification-worksheet.md) during or immediately after the
call, not from memory later.

## Tracking (kept outside this repository)

Real interview records — names, companies, quoted costs — are business- and
customer-sensitive and MUST NOT be committed to this governed, version-controlled
repository, per `SC-PROD-006`'s existing instruction. Track the funnel and the
validation-gate tally in a separate spreadsheet or document kept outside source
control. Track at minimum: contacted / responded / booked / completed, and for each
completed interview, the six `SC-PROD-006` rubric checkboxes plus whether a fixture
or pilot interest was offered.

## What this plan does not do

It does not guarantee a single response. It is a starting method to run, measure
against the real funnel numbers it produces, and revise — the sourcing channels and
message templates above are hypotheses about what will work in this segment, not
proven facts. Revise the channel mix and message wording based on what the actual
response-rate data says after the first 20-30 contacts, rather than running the
full list against an unvalidated approach.
